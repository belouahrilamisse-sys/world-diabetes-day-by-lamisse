About World Diabetes Day

Every year on November 14, the world unites to talk about diabetes. The date honors the birthday of Frederick Banting, who co-discovered insulin with Charles Best in 1921. The blue circle is the global symbol of diabetes awareness: a sign of unity, life and the worldwide diabetes community.

1921 Insulin is discovered, turning a deadly disease into a manageable one.
1991 The International Diabetes Federation and WHO create World Diabetes Day.
2006 The United Nations adopts a resolution recognizing the day; it is observed officially from 2007.
Today Millions of people join campaigns, schools and screenings under the blue circle.

Understanding Diabetes

Tap each step to follow what happens to your food. Glucose is the sugar your cells burn for energy. Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas that works like a key, letting glucose leave the blood and enter cells. In diabetes, there is too little insulin or it does not work well, so glucose stays in the blood: this is hyperglycemia.

⚖️

Diabetes vs. a temporary rise

Glucose naturally rises after a meal, or during stress or illness, then returns to normal. Diabetes means glucose stays too high over time, confirmed by laboratory tests ordered by a doctor.

Symptoms & Warning Signs

Recognizing the symptoms early can make a difference.

Some people with type 2 diabetes have few or no symptoms. If diabetes is suspected, a medical evaluation and laboratory testing are needed: signs alone cannot confirm it.

Healthy Lifestyle & Prevention

Healthy habits can reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes, but they cannot prevent every case. Type 1 diabetes is not preventable by lifestyle changes.

Complications

Over many years, high glucose can affect the body. Good care, glucose management and regular screening help reduce these risks.
